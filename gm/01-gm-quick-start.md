# GM Quick Start (read this first)

Source: Root RPG core playbook pack and GM reference, plus the *Bertram's Cove* quickstart. Check the core book if a ruling matters to you.

## The 60-second version

- It is a conversation. Players say what their vagabond does. If a **move** is triggered, follow the move. If not, keep talking.
- Dice: **2d6 + a stat**. Players roll. **You never roll.**
  - **10+** = full success
  - **7-9** = success with a cost, complication or choice
  - **6 or less** = miss. You say what happens, and it is usually bad. Your best moments come from misses.
- After every move or answer, ask: **"What do you do?"**
- Your job is to make the Woodland feel alive, make the players' vagabonds feel important, and **play to find out what happens**. You are not their opponent.

## Your three agendas

1. Make the Woodland seem large, alive, and real.
2. Make the vagabonds' lives adventurous and important.
3. Play to find out what happens. Don't plan the ending.

## Your principles (a short list for beginners)

| Principle | What it means at the table |
|---|---|
| Describe the world like a living painting | Use 2-3 senses (smell of tar and fish, creak of ropes) |
| Address the characters, not the players | "Dubois looks at *Rowan*," not "Dubois looks at Ben" |
| Be a fan of the vagabonds | Cheer for their cool ideas. Don't punish clever plans |
| Make your move but misdirect | Don't announce your move. Just narrate the consequence |
| Sometimes disclaim decision-making | "What do you think happens when you open that door?" Ask the table |
| Make the factions and their reach a constant presence | Posters, patrols, banners, rumors of Alliance and Marquisate |
| Give denizens drives and fears | Every NPC wants something and is scared of something |
| Follow the ripples of every major action | Their choices change things. Show it |
| Call upon their station and reputation | "A Marquisate soldier recognizes your name" |
| Bring danger to seemingly safe settings | A party, a market, a hospital |

## Your moves (what you do when it's your turn)

Use one when a player misses (6-), when everyone looks at you, or when the scene needs a push.

1. Inflict **injury, exhaustion, wear, depletion, or morale** (only as set up by the fiction)
2. Reveal an unwelcome truth
3. Show signs of an approaching threat
4. Capture someone
5. Put someone in a spot
6. Disrupt someone's plans and schemes
7. Make them an offer to get their way
8. Show them what a faction thinks of them
9. Turn their move back on them
10. Activate a downside of their background, reputation, or equipment
11. After every move: **"What do you do?"**

## When to call for a roll

- **Danger, uncertainty, or a clear trigger** (see the move list in `02-rules-reference.md`).
- If it's easy, boring, or no pressure, **don't roll**. Just let them do it. Searching a quiet room with time to spare is not a roll. *Reading a tense situation* is.
- If the player says what they do and it matches a move's trigger, say "That sounds like **[move name]**. Roll + [stat]."
- If nothing fits, use **Trust Fate** (roll + Luck) as the all-purpose move.

## Inflicting harm (the one thing to get right)

- Harm isn't punishment. It makes the world feel real.
- Typical: **1 or 2 exhaustion** after a hard scramble or run, **1 depletion** when gear runs low, **1 injury** from a blow, **1 wear** when equipment takes a hit.
- Armor: a character may mark **1 wear on armor instead of 1 injury** (if the armor would plausibly help).
- If a track is full and they must mark again: exhaustion -> unconscious or at your mercy; injury -> dying; depletion -> out of supplies; wear -> item breaks.

## If you get stuck (a rescue kit)

1. **Give a carrot or a stick.** Offer a job, a reward, or a threat that fits their drives. "Dubois offers you a big payday."
2. **Show a faction's fangs.** Soldiers storm in, rebels ambush, townsfolk turn on them.
3. **Target their reputations.** Threaten the good name they care about.
4. **Make an escalation** (see `04-adventure-guide.md`, "Escalation menu").
5. **Ask the players.** "What does your character notice about this?" "Who here do you recognize?"

## Beginner-friendly GM habits

- **Spotlight rotation:** after each scene, ask yourself "who haven't I turned to?" Then ask them "What do you do?"
- **Say yes, then add a cost** on a 7-9. Say yes, but make it dramatic on a miss.
- **Keep a visible countdown** (the "bomb clock") so the table feels urgency.
- **Short scenes.** If a scene stalls for 5 minutes, escalate.
- **Quiet players:** hand them a roll-worthy moment tied to their playbook.
- **Loud players:** ask the others what they do next.
- **Rules lookup:** say "I'll look it up later, here's my ruling for now" and keep moving.

## Tone and safety (2 minutes at the start)

This scenario involves a threatened bombing of a hospital and sick patients. Before play, say: "The story has danger, fear and some dark choices. If anything makes you uncomfortable, say so, and we'll tone it down or skip it." Keep gore light. Tone: adventurous swashbuckling, not grim.
